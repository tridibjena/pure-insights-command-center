## Executive summary

1. **The latency SLO is broken platform-wide, not by a few outliers.** 74.5% of report-months load slower
   than 15 s; the median load is 27.8 s and p95 is 52.1 s. In the latest snapshot
   76 of 100 reports breach. Latency is spread almost evenly from 0.6 s to the 55 s ceiling, so fixing the
   slowest handful of dashboards will not restore the SLO.
2. **More than a quarter of automated refreshes fail.** Refresh failure rate is 28.2%
   (382 FAILED, 182 TIMED_OUT); 25 reports are failing in the latest
   snapshot. Those 564 report-months carried 337,131 credits
   ($1,011,393) and $129,209 of AWS hosting — spend behind dashboards that were
   serving stale data. On a live account `cost.v_failed_query_spend` isolates the credits burned by the failed runs themselves.
3. **Orphaned reports carry $480,862 of unaccountable spend.** 94 reports
   had no owner in at least one month; 14 are orphaned right now. Orphaned report-months consumed
   141,170 credits ($423,510) plus $57,352
   of AWS hosting — the single largest "owner" in the chargeback.
4. **AWS hosting cost is disconnected from usage.** Correlation between active views and AWS cost is
   -0.027 (Spearman -0.0274). 135 report-months
   breach the 3×IQR cost-per-view fence ($2.06/view), 4 incur cost with zero
   views, and 93 report impossible usage (peak concurrent users above monthly views).
5. **Snowflake, not AWS, dominates unit cost** (at $3.00/credit). Across the portfolio a report view costs
   **$2.43 in Snowflake compute** and **$0.31 in AWS hosting**
   (fully loaded **$2.74/view**). Snowflake spend totals $3,619,057 vs AWS $461,949.
   Per-report fully loaded cost per view ranges from $1.83 to $4.99 (median $2.72).

## Forensic checks behind these findings

| Check | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Snapshots are consecutive, non-overlapping 30-day periods | No overlap between passes: each pass is an independent 30-day period, i.e. a monthly snapshot. |
| Report identity explains none of the variance | No metric differs between reports more than chance (all p ≥ 0.05): rows behave like independent draws, so per-report rankings (slowest reports, highest cost per view, decommission candidates) are not statistically distinguishable from noise in this dataset. |
| Owners — and orphaning — are assigned independently each month | Ownership and orphaning carry no month-to-month memory: the churn is random reassignment in the source data, not a pattern of hand-offs. |
| UNKNOWN refresh status is a telemetry gap, not a hidden failure | UNKNOWN is unrelated to failures or performance: it is missing telemetry scattered across the portfolio. |
| Anomalies were injected independently; overlaps are chance or arithmetic | The negative-credit/orphan overlap is exactly what chance predicts, and zero-view months carry credits because almost every row does. The only significant pairs are arithmetic: zero views make any peak exceed views, and very low views drive both the impossible-usage and the cost-per-view outlier flags. |
